The problemPlate 02 / 12

Animation is the thing every design tool puts behind the till

Not by accident. Encoding video on a server costs the vendor real money on every single click, so the incentive is to meter it — and everything downstream of that incentive shapes the product.

Consequence one

Motion is per-template, not per-layer

You pick one canned effect for the whole page. Staggering a headline against its backdrop — the thing that makes motion read as designed rather than applied — is not on offer.

Consequence two

The export is a screen capture

Cheaper to implement, and it inherits every stutter the machine had that afternoon. The file you get is not the thing you approved.

Consequence three

Resolution is the pricing lever

The editor is generous because it costs nothing. The download is where the business model lives, which is why that is where the ceiling always sits.

Who feels this. The small operator posting three times a week — a cafe, a promoter, a tutor, a market stall. Motion is what stops the scroll, and it is precisely what they cannot reach without a card on file.

The insightPlate 03 / 12

The browser got fast enough to move the render off the server

WebAssembly, WebCodecs, and modern canvas mean the expensive parts — segmentation, compositing, video encoding — now run acceptably on the machine already sitting in front of the user.

What changes for the user

The ceiling disappears

Nothing is metered because nothing is being spent. The highest export setting stops being a tier and goes back to being a setting.

What changes for the operator

Marginal cost per export is zero

Serving the application is a static file problem. A user who exports two hundred videos this month costs the same as one who exports none.

Incumbents cannot follow without cannibalising the line item that funds them. That is not a technical moat — it is a structural one, and structural moats last longer.

How it worksPlate 05 / 12

One pure function decides where everything is at any moment

Give it an animation spec, a layer, and a point in time. It returns a position, a scale, a rotation, an opacity. Nothing is stored between frames, so the same moment always resolves to the same answer.

Consumer one

The preview calls it while you scrub

Sixty times a second, against the live canvas.

Consumer two

The encoder calls it while it writes

Once per frame, deliberately, waiting for each to settle before committing it.

Because both paths run sample(spec, layer, t), the recording is a render of the design rather than a capture of a screen that happened to be showing it. This is the least glamorous file in the repository and the one thing here a competitor cannot copy in an afternoon.

The type effects follow the same rule. A neon glow is four inked passes of the same word, offset — geometry, not a filter applied afterwards. That is why it survives a 6× export instead of turning to mush.

Business modelPlate 09 / 12

Free where it costs nothing, paid where it costs something

The current build charges for nothing, and that is a deliberate position rather than an oversight. Revenue never comes from the export, because the export is the promise.

Stays free, permanently

The engine

Editor, effects, motion, every format, every resolution. It runs on the user's hardware, so it costs nothing to give away and the promise is credible.

Where money enters

Content and continuity

A curated template catalogue, brand kits, and sync across devices — things that genuinely carry a cost, and that a professional will pay for without feeling held hostage.

Later

Teams

Shared libraries and approval flows. Sold per seat to organisations, which is where design tooling revenue has always actually come from.

The discipline: an individual never hits a wall mid-task. Every paid line is additive. The moment a paywall lands between someone and their own artwork, the reason to choose this over the incumbent evaporates.

RisksPlate 11 / 12

What could sink this, stated plainly

  • Content gap
    Six templates is not a catalogue. The target user wants a finished flyer, not an empty canvas. Mitigation: the catalogue is the next body of work, and the project format is already portable JSON, so contributions do not require a schema change.
  • No lock-in
    Local files mean users can walk at any moment. Accepted knowingly. Retention has to be earned by the tool being better, which is a harder discipline and a healthier one.
  • Incumbent response
    A large vendor could ship per-layer motion. They could — but unmetered local export cannibalises the tier that funds them, which is why the gap has stayed open this long.
  • Blind metrics
    No accounts means no usage data. Real, and the direct cost of the privacy position. Addressed later with opt-in, aggregate-only telemetry, never by quietly adding an account requirement.
  • Browser variance
    Codec support differs across browsers. Handled by shipping both MP4 and WebM and selecting per capability, but it remains a support surface.
